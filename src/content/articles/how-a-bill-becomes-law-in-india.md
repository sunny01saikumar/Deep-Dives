A bill is a proposed law. It becomes an Act only after both Houses of Parliament pass it and the President gives assent.

## Introduction

An ordinary bill can start in either the Lok Sabha or the Rajya Sabha. A money bill, which deals only with taxes and government spending, can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.

## Three readings

In the **first reading** the bill is introduced. In the **second reading** members discuss it clause by clause and can propose amendments. In the **third reading** the House votes on whether to pass it. Between readings, a bill may be sent to a parliamentary committee for closer study.

## The second House

Once one House has passed the bill, it goes to the other, which follows the same steps. If the Houses disagree on an ordinary bill, a joint sitting can be called to settle it. Money bills work differently: the Rajya Sabha can suggest changes, but the Lok Sabha decides whether to accept them.

## Assent

A bill passed by both Houses goes to the President. For an ordinary bill, the President can give assent, or return it for reconsideration. If Parliament passes it again, the President must give assent. After assent, the bill is published in the Gazette and becomes an Act.

## Why the details matter

When a news report says a bill "passed", check which House it passed and which step remains. A bill passed by one House has not yet become law.
