The repo rate is the interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends money to commercial banks for the short term. The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee, which has six members, decides it at its regular meetings.

## From the RBI to your loan

Banks price floating-rate home loans against an external benchmark. For many borrowers that benchmark is the repo rate. Your loan rate is the benchmark plus a fixed spread chosen by the lender. When the repo rate changes, the benchmark changes, and your loan rate follows at the next reset date set in your agreement.

## What changes for you

When your rate goes down, you can either pay a lower EMI or keep the same EMI and finish the loan sooner. When it goes up, the reverse happens: either the EMI rises or the loan runs for longer. Lenders differ in which they choose, so your loan statement is the place to check.

## What does not change

- A fixed-rate loan stays at its agreed rate for the fixed period
- The spread added by the lender stays the same unless you renegotiate it
- Loans from some non-bank lenders may follow a different benchmark

## Questions to ask your lender

Ask which benchmark your loan uses, how often the rate resets, and whether a rate change alters your EMI or your tenure. This is general information, not financial advice.
