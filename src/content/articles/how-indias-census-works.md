The Census of India is the country's largest administrative exercise. It is run by the Registrar General and Census Commissioner, under the Ministry of Home Affairs, and held once every ten years. The current ten-year series began in 1881.

## Two stages

The census happens in two stages. In the first, enumerators list every house and record details about housing, such as the material it is built from and whether it has drinking water and electricity. In the second, they count every person and record age, sex, education, work, religion, language and more.

## Who does the counting

Millions of teachers and government staff work as enumerators, visiting households in their own areas. People are asked to give their details in person, and answers are protected by law from being used for anything other than statistics.

## What the numbers are used for

- Drawing the boundaries of constituencies
- Planning schools, hospitals and roads
- Deciding how funds and schemes are shared across states and districts
- Studying how families, work and migration are changing

## Why it is watched closely

Because so many decisions are tied to population figures, even small questions, such as which categories appear on the form, can become public debates. That is why changes to the census are covered closely whenever they are announced.
