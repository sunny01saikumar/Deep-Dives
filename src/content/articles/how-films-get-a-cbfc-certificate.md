The Central Board of Film Certification, or CBFC, is a statutory body under the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting. Under the Cinematograph Act, 1952, a film needs a certificate from the board before it can be shown in cinemas in India.

## The process

The producer applies to the board and submits the film. An examining committee watches it and recommends a category. The committee can also suggest cuts or changes. The producer can accept the suggestions, or ask for the film to be reviewed again by a revising committee.

## The four categories

| Certificate | Who can watch |
| --- | --- |
| U | Everyone |
| UA | Everyone, with parental guidance for younger children |
| A | Adults only |
| S | Specialised audiences, such as doctors or scientists |

## What the board looks at

The board checks a film against guidelines that cover violence, sexual content, language, and material that could affect public order or the country's relations with other states. Whether a particular scene crosses a line is often the subject of public argument.

## Why a certificate matters

The certificate decides who can buy a ticket and how a film can be advertised. A change in category, or a demand for cuts close to a release date, can affect when and where a film opens.
