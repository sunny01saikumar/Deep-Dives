A rocket works because of a rule worked out by Isaac Newton: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. A rocket burns fuel and throws the hot gas out of its engine at high speed. As the gas goes down, the rocket goes up.

## It does not need air

A common belief is that a rocket pushes against the air. It does not. That is why rockets work in space, where there is no air. A rocket carries its own oxygen, called the oxidiser, so its fuel can burn anywhere.

## Why rockets come in stages

Most of a rocket's weight at launch is fuel. As fuel is used up, empty tanks are dead weight. Many rockets are built in stages, and each stage is dropped when it is finished, so the remaining rocket is lighter and easier to accelerate.
